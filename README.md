The bottom line
Nassau Candy is healthy, earning 65.9% gross margin, but it depends on very few products. Five Wonka Bars produce 95.1% of profit and are made in two factories. A small tail of products earns thin margins, though it matters little in dollars. The big opportunities are protecting the core range and tuning its price and cost, not rescuing the tail.
What we found
•	Profit is concentrated. Five of 15 products give 80% of sales and 80% of profit. Scrumdiddlyumptious alone is 20.7% of profit.
•	Top sellers are not always top earners. Triple Dazzle Caramel sells the most ($28,485) yet earns 65.3% margin; Nutty Crunch sells least of the five bars but earns 71.3%.
•	Kazookles is the margin risk. It costs $3.00 to make and sells for $3.25: a 7.7% margin. It pulls the 'Other' division down to 44.8% versus 67.4% for Chocolate.
•	A quiet bright spot. Lickable Wallpaper earns $10.00 profit per unit, about four times a bar.
•	Margin comes from the product, not the place. Region, shipping mode and month barely change margin (all within about one point). Product and pricing decide profitability.
•	Supply and growth quality. Lot's O' Nuts supplies 56.5% of profit; two factories supply 95.1%. Sales grew 45% in 2025 but margin did not improve.

What we recommend

What to keep in mind
Figures are gross margins before freight, promotions and overheads. Scenarios assume sales volumes stay the same when prices change, so they are upper bounds. Several date and label fields in the source data are inconsistent and should be corrected at source; they do not change the profitability results.
